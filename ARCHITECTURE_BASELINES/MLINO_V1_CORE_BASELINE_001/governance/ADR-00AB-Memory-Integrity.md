# ADR-00AB: Memory Integrity Boundary

**Status:** Proposed Governance Extension
**Scope:** Additive Governance Layer
**Kernel Impact:** No Kernel Revision Required
**Relation:** Applies to Kernel Architecture Specification v1.2

**Governance Status:** Approved / Frozen
**Owners:** AI Governance
**Decision Category:** AI Governance / Memory Integrity
**Supersedes:** None
**Superseded by:** None

---

## Context

MLINO's governance foundation currently protects who may authorize actions (ADR-00X), how outputs are traced to their origin (ADR-00Y), who may independently verify compliance (ADR-00Z), and whether generated actions remain faithful to an authorized goal (ADR-00AA). None of these establish whether the information substrate — memory, accumulated organizational context, and stored knowledge — that decisions, interpretations, and verifications rely upon is itself valid to rely upon.

Traceability, as established by ADR-00Y, guarantees that information can be traced to its origin, source, and transformation. Traceability does not guarantee that the information is correct, current, or trustworthy. Fully traceable information can still be outdated, mistaken, or misleading. Traceability establishes where information came from; Integrity establishes whether information remains valid to rely upon. These are orthogonal properties: satisfying one does not imply the other.

Memory is not merely stored history; it functions as an active substrate for future reasoning. Interpretations of goals, actions generated by the system, information presented to human actors for authorization, and determinations made by independent validators all draw upon this substrate. If the substrate itself is compromised, every downstream process that relies upon it can produce invalid outcomes while satisfying every other governance boundary — because none of those boundaries examine the substrate itself.

This creates an architectural gap distinct from every existing ADR: a corrupted or invalid memory can distort a goal's interpretation without ADR-00AA detecting it, since ADR-00AA protects the fidelity of the interpretation process given its inputs, not the validity of those inputs. A human actor can authorize an action based on invalid information without ADR-00X detecting it, since ADR-00X protects who authorizes, not what they are shown. An independent validator can certify compliance while relying on the same invalid substrate without ADR-00Z detecting it, since ADR-00Z protects independence of judgment, not correctness of what the judgment is based on.

This ADR exists to define that missing boundary.

## Decision

Information used as a foundation for reasoning, decision-making, authorization, or verification must satisfy an integrity requirement. Information lacking established integrity may not be relied upon as a valid foundation for these purposes.

Reliance occurs whenever information influences a reasoning, decision-making, authorization, or verification outcome, regardless of whether that influence is described as provisional, partial, advisory, pending confirmation, or by any similar label. No characterization of how information is used may exempt that use from this requirement if the information influences the outcome.

Integrity does not define absolute truth of information; it defines whether information may be relied upon within the system's governance boundaries.

Traceability and Integrity are distinct architectural properties. Full compliance with ADR-00Y's traceability requirement does not, by itself, satisfy this ADR's integrity requirement, and satisfying this ADR does not diminish or replace the traceability requirement of ADR-00Y.

Integrity is temporal. Information that was valid when recorded may later become invalid. This ADR does not presume that integrity, once established, remains permanent.

Accumulated experience or learning must not silently transform unverified or invalid information into information treated as accepted organizational knowledge. The passage of time or repetition of use may never, by itself, substitute for the establishment of integrity.

Where multiple components rely on a shared memory substrate, the integrity requirement applies independently to each component's reliance. One component's prior acceptance, rejection, or treatment of a piece of information may never satisfy another component's integrity obligation for that same information.

Responsibility for establishing or maintaining the integrity of information used as a decision substrate must be architecturally assignable. This ADR does not itself assign that responsibility to any specific component or role; such assignment, when made, must be consistent with the verification independence established by ADR-00Z.

This ADR governs the validity of the substrate that reasoning, interpretation, authorization, and verification draw upon. It does not govern the fidelity of how a goal is interpreted from that substrate — that remains the exclusive domain of ADR-00AA. It does not govern who may authorize an action — that remains the exclusive domain of ADR-00X. It does not govern who may independently verify compliance — that remains the exclusive domain of ADR-00Z. It does not govern the traceability of information to its origin — that remains the exclusive domain of ADR-00Y. Neither this ADR nor any of the four preceding ADRs extends into another's domain.

## Consequences

### Positive
- Closes a foundational gap invisible to Authorization, Explainability, Verification Independence, and Goal Fidelity individually.
- Prevents decisions, interpretations, authorizations, and verifications from resting on an unexamined and potentially invalid foundation.
- Prevents gradual, experience-driven normalization of invalid information into treated-as-true organizational knowledge.
- Prevents the integrity requirement from being circumvented through labeling (provisional, partial, advisory) or through indirect reliance across components sharing a memory substrate.
- Technology-independent and applicable regardless of how memory, context, or knowledge is technically represented.
- Establishes integrity as an ongoing property rather than a one-time judgment, consistent with information that may become invalid over time.

### Negative
- Downstream processes relying on information must account for whether that information satisfies this requirement, without this ADR defining how such determination occurs.
- Adds a dependency: reasoning, authorization, and verification processes must now be able to account for whether their substrate satisfies this requirement, which the current governance foundation does not yet mechanize.
- May introduce friction where information cannot yet be confirmed to have integrity, requiring the system to treat such information as insufficient for reliance.
- Requires each component sharing a memory substrate to independently satisfy this requirement, which may introduce redundant effort across components relying on the same information.

## Deferred Decisions

- How integrity is measured or established.
- How integrity is verified in practice.
- How invalid or corrupted information is detected.
- How memory versioning or updates are handled.
- How historical information — including information that was once valid but is no longer relevant, information that was always incorrect, and information whose validity has changed over time — is architecturally distinguished.
- How responsibility for integrity is concretely assigned to a component or role.
- How this principle composes with specific Multi-Agent implementations, including how shared memory substrates are technically structured.

## Governance Layer Note

This document formalizes ADR-00AB as an **additive Governance Extension** relative to Kernel Architecture Specification v1.2. The Kernel Governance Compliance Audit found the Kernel's memory model (Sections 10–12, Ownership/Consent) has no concept of content integrity distinct from ownership or traceability — an absent capability, not a contradiction. No Kernel revision is required.
