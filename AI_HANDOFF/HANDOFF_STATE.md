NOTE: Design only - zero code and zero schema change, confirmed by git status on implementation/. The Business Context Model defines twelve entities with an entity-relationship proposal, ownership rules, a provenance model and a freshness and confidence model. It completes the eight-concept separation the owner required: Recommendation, Action, Outcome and Evaluation were already separated by ADR-0005, and this extends the same principle to Fact, Observation, Goal and Decision. Three distinctions emerged while designing that are worth reviewer time. A KPI is an organizational choice - we decided this matters - while a Signal is an observation, so a number can be a Signal without ever becoming a KPI; the earlier R8-b proposal had collapsed both under Metric. An Observation is what was recorded while a Signal is a detection over observations, and a signal may legitimately lead to no recommendation at all. And Capability is the strongest guard against irrelevant recommendations: without it the engine can tell a business with no video capability to launch a video campaign, and a recommendation the business cannot execute is noise. One real divergence from my own earlier R8-b proposal is recorded rather than resolved silently, as OD-13: UserKnowledge and InferredKnowledge were never knowledge types, they were knowledge origins, since the same statement carries the same content whether a human asserts it or the system infers it, so provenance becomes a dimension on every entity rather than two extra types. Four hard provenance rules follow, the sharpest being that an inference never returns as a Fact - it can propose one, but promotion requires human confirmation. Freshness is deliberately per-entity rather than one global number, because an Observation never goes stale as it describes the past while a Signal goes stale quickly, and expired entities are excluded from engine input yet retained for historical queries, the same invariant already implemented in ExternalWorkspaceLink.
