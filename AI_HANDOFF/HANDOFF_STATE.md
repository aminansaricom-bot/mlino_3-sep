No schema, no migration, no new entity; the phase 1 Capability contract in code is untouched and its revision is recorded only as a plan. Decision D-52: a Capability is what the business can actually do - not an offer, not availability, not visibility - and it carries four independent dimensions rather than one lifecycle: ability (planned, active, retired), audience (internal, customer_facing), verification (the existing ADR-0006 model), and publication (unpublished, published, withdrawn). Publication requires all five of: active, customer_facing, human_confirmed by an authorised role, at least one non-inferred evidence item, and fresh verification; the freshness horizon was deliberately not given a number because it depends on the industry. The decision's strongest ground is an old approved one: D-08 says consent applies when data leaves the organisation, and publishing to V2 is exactly that, so publication is a human act by an authorised role of the business and MLINO, models, modules and V2 can never publish; automatic publication of every customer-facing capability was considered and rejected on the same ground. Automation is asymmetric: withdrawal may happen automatically because it is the safe direction, publication never. Findings: the owner's example single lifecycle packs four dimensions into one state machine and cannot express a capability that is verified, internal and temporarily unavailable at once; the implemented phase 1 status "available" is itself the conflation, since the word means availability rather than ability and "not_available" merges "no longer offered" with "not right now"; three of the owner's own examples are not capabilities - appointment availability is availability, and a restaurant menu and retail products are catalogues, i.e. Offer territory; option C does not merely duplicate, it builds the mechanism for breaking principle five, because two records for one ability diverge and a published record can outlive a retired service. Verified rather than assumed: nothing outside the contract, validation and tests consumes Capability statuses, and KnowledgeItemBase has no evidence field today, which makes publication dependent on the phase 2 Evidence implementation. Less obvious rules recorded: unpublished data must not influence any V2 output including ranking, since influence is indirect disclosure; publishing a capability does not publish its evidence content, and the identity of the confirming employee is never sent to V2; the assistant must not make negative claims either - "not listed" is true, "they do not offer it" is not - the same two-directional lesson as D-50; and self-attestation must never be shown as MLINO verification, since no platform verification process exists. A correction to my own earlier entry was made plainly: in OD-29 I had framed matching free-text names as a V1 gap, but matching meaning is relevance, which the owner assigns to V2 - V1 guarantees truth and V2 decides relevance, so V1 builds no taxonomy to do V2's job, which made V1's scope smaller rather than larger.
