NOTE: Conceptual design only - zero code change, confirmed by an empty git status on implementation/ and mlino2/. Decision D-57: the hybrid model, but only the template hybrid - a permission lives only in a grant attached to a membership, and a role is merely a template that copies grants at grant time, with no live edge from role to permission. The owner's word "hybrid" was shown to carry two different meanings: the live hybrid, where roles confer permissions dynamically alongside direct grants, is the most common implementation in software and is option A in disguise - exactly what ADR-0009 forbade - while the template hybrid is D-55's "copy, not inherit". Pure direct grants were rejected for a security reason their simplicity hides: six separate grants per new staff member drift in practice into fatigue over-granting. Membership is a relation between a person and one organisation, with two states only, active and ended, following the D-24 discipline that removed speculative states; rejoining creates a new record as in D-27, and membership is point-in-time queryable as in D-28. Grants attach to the membership rather than the person, so a consultant who may publish in clinic A holds nothing in clinic B by construction. Grant basis is a closed list, founding or member_grant, deliberately excluding ADMIN_ACTION even though the workspace-link basis in D-25 includes it - a link is an identity mapping, a grant is business authority. A takeover risk was found and closed without hierarchy: grant holders are peers who cannot revoke one another's grant permission or end one another's membership; only the holder may relinquish or leave, and the last holder cannot. The cost is recorded honestly: two co-owners in dispute cannot eject each other, which is a legal matter MLINO must not adjudicate. Platform admins and AI models never receive grants, and a new rule closes the agentic form of the D-43 risk: a human may explicitly authorise a specific act on a specific object that an AI tool then executes, but a standing delegation to a machine is forbidden. The token's organisation claim is not the source of membership truth - the token proves identity, the membership record proves belonging - which also simplifies OD-08, because the token issuer need only guarantee identity. The authorization flow runs six fail-closed steps - authentication, active membership, AC-2 access, permission at that moment, act preconditions, and recording including the grant id used - and never reads the role. The central finding came from the frozen schema: V1 has no record for an organisation at all, nor for a person, member or grant; the Prisma models are ExternalWorkspaceLink, CoreEntity, EventLog, AdmissionObservability, DomainSignalProducerRegistry, OpportunityCurrentState, OpportunityInteractionState and RevenueRecoveryRawAggregate, and OrganizationId and ActorId are bare strings carried in the token, whose issuer is itself the open OD-08. So today which organisations exist and who belongs to them is defined by whoever issues tokens, which is undecided; the root of trust is undefined, hence OD-37. Minimum V1 for capability and offer publishing is an organisation record, membership, grant and revocation, founding grants and six permissions - and needs no roles at all, which is evidence that the D-55 separation genuinely works. Local discovery needs no membership for V2 users, but discovery on real V1 data waits behind this minimum because it needs published data. The requested consistency check against D-08, D-43, D-55, the AC-2 boundary and ADR-0006 found no conflict: D-08 and ADR-0006 are strengthened - confirmedBy moves from a name to authority verifiable at that moment - D-43 is extended to standing machine delegation, D-55 becomes enforceable, and AC-2 is unchanged in principle, with the permission gate separate from it and only ever narrowing.
