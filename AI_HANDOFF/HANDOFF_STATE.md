NOTE: Conceptual design only - zero code change, confirmed by an empty git status on implementation/ and mlino2/. No schema; the frozen contract and the AC-2 policy are untouched. Decision D-55: five separate concepts - role (an organisational function, a business fact), responsibility (what a role is expected to attend to, i.e. routing), permission (a standing grant to a PERSON for a governed act), authorization (the recorded act by a person who held the permission at that moment), and access (what one may see, decided by AC-2 and organisational). A role never confers permission or access: permissions belong to persons, and roles are only grant templates - copied at grant time, never inherited, so redefining a role can never silently change anyone's authority - and routing. Governed acts are a closed list in four families with different risks: attesting truth, consent to exposure, commitment, governance; recording an observation or action is not governed because provenance already makes it auditable. No implicit permission exists: the founding owner receives explicit grants at onboarding, recorded, visible and revocable. A MLINO platform admin holds no business permission ever, because acting for a business would breach D-08 (consent) and D-43 (decision); the only allowed platform act on business data is safety withdrawal, in the safe direction. No self-escalation, no lock-out of the last grant holder, and revocation never erases past authorizations - "who held the permission at time T" is an audit requirement, the D-28 invariant again. Separation of duties is permitted but not required, so a one-person clinic still works, and a future Policy can require it without changing the model because every act records its author. Two items in the instruction's list were shown to be of a different kind: executing an action is responsibility rather than permission, and receiving a recommendation is routing; and "approving capability publication" is two acts - attesting the capability is real (confirmedBy) and consenting to its exposure (publishedBy), which may be two different people. The central finding came from the code: three unrelated "role" concepts coexist - the frozen token role (owner_manager, receptionist_coordinator), the eleven TARGET_ROLES, and the untyped Capability.ownedByRoles - and the first two do not overlap at all, so can_change_status: TargetRole[] in the recommendation contract speaks a vocabulary no logged-in actor carries, and "who may accept a recommendation" cannot be evaluated today. A positive finding grounded the whole decision: V1's read path already implements role-is-not-access - IC-14 runs AC-2 organisationally first and applies the role only afterwards as intended_audience, which the AC-2 port itself classes as "not a Governance decision", and AC-2 policy section 4.1 names the consumer as the organisation, "not a role" - so D-55 generalises an existing pattern rather than inventing one. On the original OD-28: the eleven categories move to a neutral module used by both the business context and the recommendation contract without inverting dependencies; ownedByRoles becomes typed and means responsibility; the frozen token role is neither a business role nor a permission, is not extended, has nothing built on it, and no mapping between the two vocabularies was invented. An explicit refinement of D-54 was flagged in the recommendation document: can_change_status is removed from v1.1, visibility.roles is routing rather than access, and decided_by must hold the decision permission at the time. Recorded honestly: the model is not enforceable yet, since V1 has no membership table and active membership today means a valid token - hence OD-36. ADR-0009 was written because this is a new principle, not an extension of an existing one.
