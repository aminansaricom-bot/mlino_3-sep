Option A is recorded as D-49 with six rules: Decision.basedOn may reference an EvaluationRecord; never an OutcomeRecord and never via Evidence; by identifier only, with no import of the EvaluationRecord shape into business context; citing an evaluation counts as recorded_context; the decision's stance toward the evaluation stays in rationale rather than a typed relation (D-34); and implementation is blocked on OD-32. The central finding came from reading the implemented contract rather than trusting the framing: option A as posed cannot be implemented, because EvaluationRecord has no identifier of its own - its only key is recommendation_id, so two evaluations of the same recommendation cannot be told apart and there is nothing to reference. It also carries no provenance, although reason three of ADR-0005 itself separated evaluation out precisely to preserve its provenance. And effective is a single boolean that merges two different claims, effectiveness (did the KPI move toward the goal) and attribution (did our action cause it) - in the owner's own example, response time could have fallen because of a slow season rather than the action, so the owner's principle that causality is never claimed without evaluation is currently breakable INSIDE the evaluation itself. The owner's stated reason for rejecting option B was sharpened rather than repeated: "an outcome is only measurement" is not sufficient, since an Observation is also only measurement and Decision may cite it; the real reason is that an OutcomeRecord is recorded under a recommendation_id and so carries an attribution claim with it. Option B's apparent simplicity is also illusory, because OutcomeRecord has no identifier either. Option C contradicts D-48, recorded one pass earlier. A complication absent from the framing was resolved rather than ignored: option A is the first reference from the business-context layer into the recommendation lifecycle layer, which looks like the dependency D-40 rejected, but a loop by definition returns from downstream to upstream, and D-40 forbids shape coupling, not identifier references. Outside OD-31 but directly on the owner's stated goal: ActionRecord makes recommendation_id mandatory, so actions arising from the business's own decisions are never recorded or learned from, a selection bias; wrapping them as Recommendations would fabricate records no engine produced. On cross-business learning: it can stay future scope, it almost certainly needs a new concept because an aggregated insight has no single businessId, and it does not change V1 architecture today, with one condition - OD-32 - since aggregating unattributed boolean evaluations across businesses would present correlations of unverified claims as findings, violating principle two at scale.
