Decision D-56: availability is forward-looking, perishable, contended operational state - whether a specific capability or offer can be obtained at a specific time or quantity - and reading it does not reserve it, so availability is never a promise; only a reservation is. On the owner's question of category, the answer is none of the three offered: not standing business truth, not event knowledge (which is always about the past), not experience data, but a third category, operational state; it is not a knowledge type, by the same logic D-45 applied to Evidence, and once its moment passes it becomes at most an observation. Ownership has three layers that must not be merged: truth lives in the business's system of record or its direct declaration, V1 owns the governed representation inside MLINO, and V2 owns only presentation - MLINO is a governed relay, not the ledger, the same import pattern D-53 used for offers. It references a capability (appointment slots) or an offer (stock, bundle seats), is visible only for published targets, and never comes from AI_INFERRED or from customer behaviour, which rules out any "usually quiet at this hour" pattern under D-36. Expired, unreachable or revoked-link availability collapses to "unknown", never to the last known value, and only ever in the safe direction. Hours were separated three ways: regular hours are a Fact, planned exceptions are a time-bounded Fact, and live state is availability; V2 may say "within declared hours" but never a bare "open now", because the former states what is known and the latter asserts live state that is not. What V2 can say today is the published capability and offer, offer validity phrased as validity, declared hours, and a hand-off to the business's own published booking channel - hand-off is not a claim. Three findings came from the code: V2 makes no availability claims today and contract 02 has no hours, slot or stock fields, so the D-52 fail-closed rule is genuinely holding, unlike the Offer case where V2 had run ahead; V1's capacity engine F-01 already reads the raw material, working hours and booked minutes, but only to tell the business "today is emptier than usual" - same data, two concepts, two correctness bars - and its repository is in-memory, so no real availability source exists; and the most dangerous path was in none of the three options - MLINO knows when a clinic has spare capacity, and telling V2 users "go now, they have room" would both leak internal intelligence and present inference as availability. The correct bridge uses only already-approved links: capacity signal, a recommendation to the business (D-54), the business creating and publishing an offer (D-51, D-53, D-52) - the business chooses what leaves. Options A and B were rejected on a mechanical ground as well as a conceptual one: capabilities and offers are versioned, so availability inside a capability would mint a new version per booking, and inside an offer would make every sale an Action under D-53 rule 10. A direct V2 read from the business's booking system was rejected because it would bypass V1 governance and the D-08 consent, while a link out to the business's own channel is allowed because it asserts nothing. A Persian vocabulary hazard was recorded: "availability" and "access" differ by a single suffix in Persian, and both terms were added to the vocabulary table.
